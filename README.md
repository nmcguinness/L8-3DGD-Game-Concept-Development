# Game Concept Development
## Collaborative Project — Stage 4

# Overview

This folder supports two sessions that take you from an individual game concept to a team plan.

1. **Concept development (individual, in pairs).** A one-hour session in which you strengthen your individual game concept before submitting the Moodle concept questionnaire. It targets three common weaknesses: a theme that does not reach the **mechanic**, a concept that **plays it safe**, and a player character with no **wants, limits or history**.
2. **Studio kick-off (team).** A one-hour lab, held at the end of Sprint 2 of Phase 1, in which your team plans its project like a small games studio: roles and a working agreement, design pillars, an hours budget, technical research, a quality-first content budget, a roadmap against the CP project calendar, and Sprint 3. It is built on two rules: **research before you build**, and **fewer assets, each at your best**.

# Session 1: Concept development

## Session plan

| Time | Activity | File |
| :- | :- | :- |
| 0:00–0:15 | Note delivered as a short lecture | Note |
| 0:15–0:50 | Exercises 01–08 in pairs, with Checkpoints 1–3 | Lab |
| 0:50–1:00 | Exercise 09: findings report, then two or three 1-minute summaries | Lab, form |

## How to complete the lab

1. Read the note before or during the lecture. Keep it open during the lab.
2. Follow **How to run** in the lab. Create your own `concept_worksheet.md` from the **Setup (copy once)** block.
3. Attempt each exercise before opening its **Solution** dropdown.
4. Show the lecturer your outputs at each checkpoint before moving on.
5. Complete the findings report with your partner and submit it.
6. Update your own Moodle questionnaire with your revised core verb, chosen variant and character spine. The questionnaire stays anonymous; the findings report does not.

# Session 2: Studio kick-off

## Choose your route

| Route | For teams who… | Steps |
| :- | :- | :- |
| **A: your game concept** | Already have an agreed concept | 1–8, then share-out |
| **B: a new proposal** | Need a direction: a partner project (Wing Chun or Alzheimer's VR) or a team concept that combines or re-scopes members' submissions | 0–6, then share-out |

## Studio kick-off plan

| Step | Route A (min) | Route B (min) |
| :- | :- | :- |
| 0. Choose your direction | – | 10 |
| 1. Studio setup: major and minor roles, working agreement | 6 | 6 |
| 2. Pitch, pillars and non-goals | 6 | 6 |
| 3. Hours budget | 4 | 4 |
| 4. Research the risks: research cards and spikes | 10 | 10 |
| 5. Scope (MoSCoW) and content budget | 10 | 10 |
| 6. Roadmap against the CP project calendar | 6 | 6 |
| 7. Pipeline check: GitHub or Diversion, Unreal, Blender, FMOD | 5 | – |
| 8. Sprint 3 goal and backlog | 5 | – |
| Share-out (60 seconds per team) | 5 | 5 |
| **Total** | **57** | **57** |

## Before you start

- Whole team present, with one laptop on the team repository and one on shared notes.
- Route A: your game concept. Route B: both partner briefs, plus your team's submitted concepts and their panel feedback.
- The CP project calendar, open, with its ICA and GCA deliverables.
- A timekeeper. Every step has a hard time box.

## How to complete the studio kick-off

1. Create `docs/studio_plan.md` (template in Section 7 of the lab) and `docs/working_agreement.md` (template in Appendix A) in your team repository.
2. Work through the steps for your route, in order. Move on when the time box ends.
3. Meet each step's **Checkpoint** before moving on.
4. Answer the starred working-agreement questions today; finish the rest before the end of Sprint 3.
5. Use Appendix B for asset naming from the first asset, and Appendix C to test your pillars.
6. Commit both files. `studio_plan.md` is a living document: revisit it at every sprint review.
